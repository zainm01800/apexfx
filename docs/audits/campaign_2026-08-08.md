## PART 1 — CODE AUDITS (4 agents, ~whole-engine coverage)

### 1A. Risk & sizing core — CRITICAL finding: the frozen proof reads live config

- **C1 (CRITICAL):** `run_paper_portfolio.py` builds `RiskManager` from `get_config()` every night — the frozen proof silently tracks live config edits. The proof is currently running positions sized under **three different risk-cap regimes**: 2% (17 Jul seed era) → 1% (19 Jul, recorded in pre-reg change log #3) → **0.75% (23 Jul — NOT recorded in the pre-reg change log)**. Per the contract's own rule (f), unrecorded config changes breach the freeze. **The risk_abs dispersion I previously attributed to the Bayesian sizer was wrong — there is NO Bayesian sizer in the frozen path at all** (it's daemon-only). The dispersion is the cap changes + regime_scale + portfolio-cap squeeze.
  - *Fix queued:* pin the effective RiskConfig in code next to the pinned universe; add a test asserting the pinned values; record the 23 Jul change in the pre-reg §6.
- **M1:** the paper stepper calls `TradeManager.update_position` **without `open_=`** (`paper.py:337-349`) → the proof books stop exits **at the stop price even through gaps**, while the certified backtest fills at the worse open. The proof is systematically more optimistic than its own certified backtest.
- **M2:** daily-loss stop is a latent no-op in the paper path (no `day_start_equity`, no flatten step) — moot today (limit unset) but dangerous if the knob is ever turned.
- **m3/m4 (daemon-only):** one NaN pnl permanently neuters payoff adaptation per instrument; cold-start bypasses the Kelly edge gate.
- **Verified correct:** TradeManager core (stop-before-target, monotonic trails, no double-counted partials), circuit breaker (stateless, self-resetting), daily_stop anchoring.

### 1B. Execution & mirror — CRITICAL findings

- **Orphaned brackets root cause:** cancel paths exist (`_cancel_brackets`, post-run sweep) but are gated behind `attach_stops`, which is **off in the cron path**; `close_position()` has no resting-order teardown. ### 1D. Learning loop — CRITICAL findings (dormant daemon path)

## PART 2 — DEEP RESEARCH (5 workstreams)

### 2A. Earnings blackout — family already dead; the live losses wouldn't have been caught

- **The exact idea was gated 2026-07-24 and REJECTED** (±1d all-entries veto: Sharpe 0.9955→0.8522, worst day worsened). It's on the auto-researcher's dead-end list.
- **Counterfactual honesty check:** both live gap losses (MSFT, PLTR) were entered **2026-07-17**, 9–12 trading days BEFORE their events. No 48/72/96h *entry* veto catches them. The pain is held-into-earnings, not entered-before-earnings.
- Literature agrees: the earnings-announcement premium (Frazzini & Lamont; Barber et al.) and earnings-momentum entanglement (Chordia & Shivakumar 2006) mean entry vetoes amputate edge, not just tails. Stops can't stop jumps (Kaminski & Lo 2014).
- **Mechanistically-correct follow-up (for graduation):** de-risk OPEN positions the bar before a held name reports (flatten/halve) — an exit-side rule, untested, needs its own pre-reg.

### 2B. Entry timing (overnight vs intraday) — the book pays the gap; worth ~£230/mo

- The book fills **next-open** already (verified in code + pre-reg §execution convention).
- Literature (Lou/Polk/Skouras JFE 2019: momentum premia accrue overnight; Aboody JFQA 2018; Bogousslavsky JFE 2021): for a momentum book, next-open entries systematically pay the gap-up.
- **Measured on the certified trade set (this campaign, E4-lite):** signed entry delta = **+£23,423 over 1,637 trades (mean +£14.31/trade)**, concentrated in single-name equity longs (+£24,973 ≈ **£231/mo ≈ 13% of the book's monthly expectancy**). Above the £5/trade immateriality bar → a proper MOC-fill gate is justified at graduation (operational change: MOC orders with near-close signal computation).

### 2C. Short-side conditioning — the measurement overturned the research no-go

- Research verdict (literature + factor-gate precedent 0/15): NO-GO for an index-conditioned short veto. **Then the measurement ran and flipped the call** — see Part 3, E2. The book's own data says bull-regime shorts are the bleeder (−£66.67/trade) while bear-regime shorts are ~flat and keep the crash payoff. Literature caveat stands (CGH: cross-sectional shorts earn in up-markets) — which is exactly why this went to a gate instead of adoption.
- **Unconditional finding:** the short sleeve is net-negative over the certified backtest (−£21,087 over 401 trades; every short class negative except ETFs +£2,316).

### 2D. Prop-firm risk geometry — the recommended firm CHANGED

- **The5ers "Pro Growth" as modeled (6%/5%/5%) no longer exists** — the 82.6% pass figure is moot. Current best structural fits: **The5ers High Stakes 100k** (10%+5%, 5% daily, 10% static, no consistency rule — the book's worst day −3.61% never threatens it) or **FTMO 1-Step** (r150_cap045, 62.6% MC pass, ONLY with the 2.5% engine daily stop enforced). **Maven is disqualified (EAs banned)**; its 20% funded consistency rule is also structurally incompatible with lumpy trend P&L (median window runs 28–37% best-day share).

- FT+ consistency (35% challenge): ~⅓–½ of fast passes need a 2–4-week dilution tail — costs time, not the account.

### 2E. FX sleeve on swap-free accounts — the engine's own evidence says drop these pairs

- USD/JPY and USD/CAD were the **two worst majors at the July re-gate** (Sharpe −0.28 / −0.58, 0% positive CPCV paths); the designated FX book was AUD/USD + NZD/USD only. The live book taking them contradicts the ledger.
- Swap-free matrix: **FT+ and Maven are the only genuinely free swap-free firms** (no admin fee); FundingPips charges +10% + doubled commissions and excludes crypto; FTMO fails swap-free + weekend-holding for 1–4-week holds.

- Silver lining: the backtester credits no swap income — its cost model already matches a swap-free account exactly.
